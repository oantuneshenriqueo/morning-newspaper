# Writing

Write everything in the reader's language (`config.language`). The paper sounds like a good newsroom of a 1980s–90s printed daily: clear, direct sentences, no internet slang, no exclamation marks, no emoji. A light touch of humor is welcome in the extras and the footer, never in the news.

## Headline
- Up to 14 words, present tense, main fact first.
- Deck (subtitle, in italics): one sentence with a consequence the sources state. Never advice or an order ("readers should watch out for scams").
- Text: 3 to 4 sentences. The first answers what, who and when. The last says what the reader can do or what comes next, when there is something.

## Section stories
- Title up to 12 words.
- Text: 1 to 2 sentences. With an empty summary there is only the title: say its fact in other words and add nothing (no "initial" problems when the title says problems).
- The source link ("Source: outlet") is added by the script.

## Copyright (mandatory)
- Write everything in your own words. Do not copy sentences from the articles, and do not just swap a word here and there.
- At most one literal quote per source, under 15 words, in quotation marks, and only when the exact wording matters.
- Never rebuild an article paragraph by paragraph: summarize the essentials and send the reader to the source.

## Today's Column (agenda)
- One appointment per line: time in the reader's usual format (e.g. "8:00" or "08h00") and the event as the reader would say it, short.
- All-day event: the `all_day` label of their language.
- Empty day: leave the agenda list empty; the template prints the "free agenda" line.
- Preparation note (optional, at most one): about something early tomorrow worth preparing today.

## Classifieds (emails)
- Written like a classified ad: a capitalized tag (the template's `wanted_reply` or `resolved` label), then one sentence.
- The sentence says who asked, when and what, in your own words, without copying the subject line.
- No amounts of money, codes, passwords or health data.

## Never
- Invent a story, number, date, source or quote.
- Add facts from memory: every name, number and detail must come from the collected title/summary or from a search made in this run. If it is not there, leave it out. The usual slips: a nationality, a comparison ("faster than before"), an adjective ("initial", "historic") and a conclusion ("proof that…").
- Give investment advice.
- Order the reader around ("you must…"), preach, or apologize ("I couldn't find much"). A slow day is a slow day.
- Narrate the process ("I searched several sources…").
